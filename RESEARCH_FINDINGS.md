# Market research — UK gas & heating trades, August 2026

Research done via web search on 2 August 2026. Sources are linked inline.
Figures from vendor marketing pages are flagged as such; treat them as
indicative rather than audited.

## Verdict: the market is right, the list needs cutting

Domestic gas and heating engineers are a strong fit for AI receptionist and
lead-response products, for reasons that are measurable rather than assumed:

- **HVAC and home services have the highest missed-call rate of any sector at
  62%.** ([getaira.io](https://www.getaira.io/blog/missed-business-calls-statistics))
- **85% of new customers never call back after an unanswered call**, so a
  missed call is a permanently lost job, not a deferred one.
  ([paperclip.co.uk](https://www.paperclip.co.uk/missed-business-phone-calls-uk/))
- Sole traders miss **35-40% of calls during peak hours**.
- A plumber missing 15 genuine prospect calls a month loses roughly **£2,400
  a month**; with referrals, annual losses are put at **£38,000-£45,000**.
  ([madeforbuilders.com](https://madeforbuilders.com/en-GB/blog/the-real-cost-of-missed-calls-for-tradespeople/))

Against a £45-99/month product, that is a payback measured in one recovered
job. No other segment in the 176-row list has this shape: industrial
fabricators, fuel distributors and oil-and-gas operators have essentially no
urgent demand, almost no mobile numbers, and buy through procurement.

**66 of 176 rows qualify. The other 110 should be dropped.**

## Timing: eight weeks, and the clock is the pitch

UK heating engineers are busiest **October to March**, and search demand for
"boiler breakdown" and "heating engineer" spikes from October through February.
June to August is their off-peak.
([leads2trade.co.uk](https://leads2trade.co.uk/news/why-installers-need-to-act-now-to-capitalise-on-autumn-and-winter-boiler-demand/),
[homekog.co.uk](https://homekog.co.uk/boiler-advice/when-is-the-best-time-to-install-a-boiler/))

This cuts both ways and determines the whole approach:

- **Good**: right now they answer the phone, have time to talk, and can be
  onboarded without disrupting a full diary.
- **Bad**: their pain is at its annual minimum, so "you're missing calls"
  lands weakly in August.
- **Therefore**: sell the coming rush, not the current pain. *"You've got
  about eight weeks before the phone doesn't stop. Do you want this running
  before October, or do you want to be setting it up in the middle of it?"*

Selling in August for an October problem is the correct play, and it expires.
By November these prospects are too busy to take the call at all.

## Compliance: screen against TPS and CTPS before dialling

This is a legal requirement, not best practice.

Under the Privacy and Electronic Communications Regulations 2003 (PECR),
unsolicited live marketing calls to numbers on the Telephone Preference
Service (TPS) or Corporate Telephone Preference Service (CTPS) are prohibited
without prior consent from that specific business.
([ICO](https://ico.org.uk/for-organisations/direct-marketing-and-privacy-and-electronic-communications/guide-to-pecr/electronic-and-telephone-marketing/telephone-marketing/))

The trap for this particular list:

- **Sole traders and most partnerships count as "individual subscribers"** and
  must be screened against **TPS**, not CTPS.
  ([RD Marketing](https://rdmarketing.co.uk/knowledge-hub/do-i-need-to-check-tps-and-ctps-before-calling/))
- Limited companies are screened against **CTPS**.
- **Both registers must be checked** — one does not cover the other.
- There is no small-business exemption; the obligation applies to a sole
  trader making cold calls as much as to a call centre.

The top 20 is dominated by mobile numbers belonging to owner-operators, which
is exactly the TPS category. Screen the 66 qualified numbers through a TPS/CTPS
checker before Monday. This is a cheap, one-off step and the alternative is an
ICO complaint.

(vendor sites, August 2026):

| [Clara](https://heyitsclara.com/gb/blog/best-ai-phone-answering-service-uk-tradespeople/) | £49.99/mo | Solo plan, UK voice |
| [ARROW](https://aiphonecalls.co.uk/) | £99/mo | Trades-focused |
| Traditional human answering services | £100-400/mo | The incumbent being displaced |

Implications:

- **£45-50/month is the anchor.** Pricing above it needs a reason: setup done
  for them, trade-specific configuration, a website included, or booking
  integration.
- Competing on price against £45 is a losing game for a one-person operation.
  The defensible edge is doing the work *for* them — these prospects will not
  self-serve onboard.
- The strongest bundle is **website + call answering**, because a meaningful
  share of this list has no real website (see below) and no competitor at £45
  is going to build one.

## Objection handling: lead with voicemail, not with AI

- **89% of people want to be able to reach a human**, and **64% of customers
  would prefer companies didn't use AI for service at all** (Gartner 2024).
  ([abby.com](https://www.abby.com/blog/risks-of-ai-receptionist/),
  [nexomize.com](https://nexomize.com/do-customers-hate-ai-receptionists/))
- The reframe that survives this: customers don't hate AI, they hate voicemail,
  hold music and being ignored. The comparison is never "AI versus you" — it is
  **"AI versus the voicemail they currently get"**.
- Expect the objection *"my customers want to speak to me"*. The answer:
  "They do — and right now, at 7pm, they get your voicemail and ring the next
  engineer. This just takes the details and texts them to you."

## The sharpest hook: they advertise 24/7 and one person answers

Every one of the four top-ranked companies checked by hand advertises
round-the-clock availability while running on a single personal mobile:

| Company | Reviews | Claim found | Website |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| HydroGreen Heating | 543 | "open 24 hours" | `hydrogreenheatinga.wixsite.com` — **free Wix subdomain** |
| Pipe Guys (Bham) | 409 | "24/7 emergency line" | `pipeguys.co.uk` — own domain, real site |
| Blaymires Plumbing | 267 | "24 hours a day, 7 days a week" | `blaymires-plumbing-heating-1.ueniweb.com` — **UENI subdomain** |
| Secure Gas 247 | 198 | "24 hours seven days a week" | `securegas247.co.uk` — own domain |

That is the opening line, and it is verifiable rather than speculative:

> *"Your website says 24/7 — who picks it up at 2am when you're asleep?"*

It is uncomfortable in the right way: they made the promise publicly, and both
of you know they cannot personally keep it.

**Caveat on the data:** only two of these four show any 24/7 or emergency
wording in the CSV's `Services` column, so the pipeline's own count (4 across
all 176 rows) badly understates it. The claim lives on their website and Google
listing, not in the source data. Check the Google listing before each call.

## Website quality: two of the four verified are on free subdomains

HydroGreen (543 reviews) is on a `wixsite.com` subdomain and Blaymires (267
reviews) on a `ueniweb.com` subdomain. Neither owns its domain. Both are
substantial, busy businesses presenting through a free page builder.

That is a website sale sitting next to the receptionist sale, and it is why the
bundle beats the £45 point product. The enrichment pipeline already flags this
pattern via `WEAK_HOSTING_SUFFIXES`; a networked stage 1 run will find the rest.

## What this changes about the plan

1. **Cut to the 66.** Ignore Aberdeen oil and gas entirely.
2. **Screen TPS and CTPS first.** Before any dialling.
3. **Check each prospect's Google listing for a 24/7 claim** — the CSV misses
   most of them, and it is the best hook available.
4. **Sell the October rush,** not today's missed calls.
5. **Price at or above £45-50 and justify it with done-for-you setup**, or
   bundle a website for the ones on free subdomains.
6. **Never frame it as AI replacing them.** Frame it as replacing voicemail.
