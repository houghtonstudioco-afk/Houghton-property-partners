# Verdicts on the sectors and companies you asked about

Researched 2 August 2026. Measured against the four filters that decide whether
a lead is worth your time: can they pay, is one person enough to say yes, can
you reach them, and does the pain cost four figures.

---

## TLT Solicitors — no

Not because they wouldn't benefit. Because of what they are.

- **£200m+ revenue**, crossed that barrier in July 2026
- **~2,200 staff** across Bristol, London, Manchester, Birmingham, Glasgow,
  Edinburgh, Belfast and Piraeus
  ([Legal Cheek](https://www.legalcheek.com/firm/tlt/), [ZoomInfo](https://www.zoominfo.com/c/tlt-llp/38387781))

That is roughly **twelve times the top of the band** agencies sell into. A firm
that size has an innovation function, existing legal-tech vendors, and a
procurement process. Your email reaches a shared inbox monitored by someone with
no authority to buy.

The deeper problem is that they are not short of the thing you sell. Legal is
the **second-highest AI-adopting sector in the UK at 29.2%**, and adoption
concentrates in the larger firms. TLT has almost certainly already bought.

## JLL — no, and the evidence is unusually concrete

- **Fortune 500, $16.3bn revenue, 90,000+ staff, 80+ countries**
- Vendor onboarding runs through **Aravo**, a third-party risk platform.
  Suppliers must complete due diligence including an NDA, **Certificates of
  Insurance** and a **Risk Assessment Profile** before they can be paid at all
  ([JLL vendor information](https://www.jll.com/en-us/vendors))

You cannot cold-email your way into that. Entry is by invitation from a JLL
employee, and then a compliance gauntlet. For a one-person business the
insurance and information-security requirements alone are likely disqualifying
before any commercial conversation happens.

## Aon — no, same category

Global insurance broker at comparable scale to JLL: tens of thousands of staff,
tens of billions in revenue, listed, with a formal supplier programme. Same
answer for the same reasons.

### What the building actually gets you

Sharing a building is worth something, but not what it looks like. It is a
**warm relationship**, not a shorter sales cycle. The person you can reach —
the on-site building or facilities manager — has no budget authority for
software, and the person who does has never heard of you.

If you want to use it: talk to people, be known, and treat any of these three as
a **12-month relationship play**, not this quarter's pipeline. Do not spend a
week writing a proposal for JLL while there are clinics that will answer in two
days.

## Cleaning companies — no, the economics don't work

This is the one I would have guessed wrong on too. The numbers are unambiguous:

- **83% are micro-businesses** with fewer than 10 staff
- **Only 1% turn over more than £5M** — 196 firms out of 20,369 nationally
- **Wages consume 50–70% of revenue**
- Competitive tendering compresses commercial operators to **10–20% net**, and
  some £15–20M franchise operators report **1–2% pre-tax**
  ([Firmbase](https://firmbase.co/resources/industry-lists/commercial-cleaning-companies-in-the-uk),
  [CleanerHQ](https://cleanerhq.com/understanding-your-cleaning-business-profit-margins/))

A £1M cleaning company might make £150K EBITDA. Your mid band at £750–1,500/mo
is £9–18K a year, which is **6–12% of their entire profit**. That is not a
price objection you can talk your way past.

There is real pain there — rota gaps, absence cover, staff churn — but it sits
on the wrong side of a labour-dominated cost base. The 1% above £5M are big
enough to pay and also big enough to have procurement.

## Recruitment agencies — yes, you were right

Already in your list and worth expanding. Placement fees run to five figures, so
one extra placement pays for a year. The screening use case is concrete enough
to demo in a first call, and the REC reports most recruitment professionals are
already using AI to cut admin — meaning **budget exists and the concept needs no
selling**.

## Insurance brokers — yes, and better than cleaning

The sector I would add in cleaning's place:

- Independent brokers carry a **29–39 hour weekly admin burden**, with **8–10
  hours on email alone**
- Revenue is **commission and renewals**, so they already think in recurring
  terms and understand what an hour reclaimed is worth
- FCA-regulated, so compliance admin is heavy and non-negotiable
- Owner-led independents are common and reachable
  ([Humbrela](https://www.humbrela.com/blog/the-hidden-cost-of-administrative-work-how-insurance-brokers-lose-50k-annually),
  [Unitary](https://www.unitary.ai/articles/ai-automation-insurance-brokers))
