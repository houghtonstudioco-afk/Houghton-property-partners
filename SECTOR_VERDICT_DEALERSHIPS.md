# Car dealerships — I was wrong to drop them

Researched 2 August 2026. I dropped this sector earlier on reachability alone
and did not check the need data. Having checked it, the need is the strongest
of any sector looked at so far.

## The numbers

- **56% of dealership leads arrive after business hours.** The highest
  out-of-hours share found in any sector, including private healthcare.
- **43.2% of dealership sales leads are mishandled** — missed calls, lapsed
  follow-up, or slow response.
- **14.1% are never logged into the CRM at all.**
- Responding within five minutes makes a business **100× more likely to
  connect** than waiting thirty (Harvard Business Review).
- **76% of dealership executives plan to increase AI budgets** for sales and
  lead response in 2026.
- **55% of dealerships using AI reported a 10–30% profit increase** within
  twelve months.

([Car Dealer Magazine](https://cardealermagazine.co.uk/how-automotive-ai-is-transforming-uk-dealership-performance/310201),
[Gnosari](https://gnosari.com/blog/car-dealership-after-hours-leads),
[Flai](https://www.useflai.com/blog/dealership-customer-experience-statistics))

Budget exists, is growing, and the pain is measurable. That clears filters 1, 2
and 4 comfortably.

## But do not say "£200,000 lost"

This is the part that matters most, because it decides whether the first thirty
seconds of a call land or not.

A dealer does not lose £200,000 when a Porsche enquiry goes elsewhere. They
lose **the gross profit on that car**. UK used-car gross margins run **12–15%
of proceeds**, and gross profit per vehicle typically sits in the **low-to-mid
single-digit percentage of retail price**
([Hilton Car Supermarket](https://www.hiltoncarsupermarket.co.uk/blog/how-uk-car-dealers-earn-from-selling-new-and-used-cars),
[VantaInsights](https://vantainsights.com/insights/used-car-dealership-profit-margins)).

So a £200,000 Porsche is roughly **£10,000–£20,000 of gross profit**, not
£200,000.

That distinction is not pedantry. A dealer principal thinks in gross-per-unit
every single day and knows exactly how thin it is. Quote them £200,000 and you
have identified yourself as someone who has never sold a car, in one sentence,
before you have said anything useful. It is the fastest possible way to lose a
room.

**The honest version is still excellent:**

> "If one enquiry a month goes cold overnight and that car was a £200k
> Porsche, that's somewhere north of ten grand of gross walking to another
> dealer. What does that look like across a year?"

Same force, and it says you understand their business.

## The real obstacle: they publish no email addresses

This is why I dropped them, and it is still true. Searching prestige dealers
returns showrooms, phone numbers and AutoTrader listings, and no addresses.
Enquiries deliberately route through portals and web forms, because that is how
dealers track lead source and attribution.

Ways round it, best first:

1. **LinkedIn to the Dealer Principal or General Manager.** These are named,
   findable people at a level that can decide. Better than an inbox anyway.
2. **Walk in.** Prestige showrooms are quiet mid-week and the sales manager is
   on the floor. A ten-minute conversation beats fifty emails, and it suits a
   local operator far more than a national vendor.
3. **Phone mid-morning Tuesday to Thursday**, avoiding weekends, which are
   their trading peak.
4. **Run the pipeline** over dealer names to pull addresses from their sites,
   which will find some that search does not surface.

## One caveat worth holding

That 76% budget figure cuts both ways. Vendors like Impel are already selling
AI lead-response into automotive hard, and dealer groups get pitched constantly.
This is a sophisticated, well-served market, not virgin ground. The independents
and small groups are where a solo operator competes — not the Sytners of the
world, who are enterprise procurement all over again.

## Verdict

**Pursue it, with the corrected pitch and a non-email channel.** On need it is
the best sector found. On reachability it is the worst. Those cancel out to
something worth a serious try, but not by mass email.
